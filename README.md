## Can Workforce Training Soften the Scarring Effect?

Workforce training programs are an affordable and accessible way for workers to develop new skills and improve their labor market prospects. This may be particularly important for workers who lose their employment: job loss has been shown to have a long-lasting scarring effect on future earnings and employment, but workers who are able to build their human capital after becoming unemployed may be able to avoid some of these impacts. Existing evaluations of workforce training programs generally use administrative data, and as a result suffer from an important shortcoming of such datasets: they typically contain total earnings, which are the product of labor supply (hours) and a wage. This is a serious limitation, since the impact of a training program depends upon which margin it operates; as we demonstrate, a positive impact on earnings is not even sufficient to show that an intervention is welfare improving. Recognizing that earnings (the product of hours and wages) places bounds on the range of each component, we utilize machine learning tools and auxiliary data to estimate the joint distribution of hours and wages conditional on earnings and a sparse set of shared covariates. We demonstrate that our method performs well by presenting three out-of-sample (and out-of-dataset) validation exercises replicating previous results. Finally, we apply this method to administrative data from Maryland to study the impact of workforce training programs on the wages and hours of workers who lose their jobs in the COVID-19 recession, overcoming a limitation of previous such analyses.
